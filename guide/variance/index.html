Sources of variation (Fig. 2A)

var = design.variance(permutations=999, pvca_bins={"age": [0, 10, 20, ...]}) · vf.plots.variance(var)

What it answers

How much of the variation in the features does each variable explain: the batch, the label, the covariates?

How it works

  • PVCA: variance components of a mixed model fitted to the leading PCs, as in pvca::pvcaBatchAssess. Continuous variables are binned (pvca_bins, or pvca_n_bins equal groups).
  • PERMANOVA, as vegan::adonis2: each variable's share of the total variation (R²), alone and adjusted for all others, with a permutation p-value

Reading the result

  • A label that is large alone but small adjusted shows up mainly through what it is tied to, usually the batch
  • Controls should sit near zero
  • PVCA shares and PERMANOVA R² answer different questions and don't add up
  • Zaslavsky (TRB, V genes): the batch takes 64% of the variance (PVCA) and 39% of the total variation alone (PERMANOVA R²), 12% adjusted. With depth (log10_clonotypes) as a technical variable, the batch's PVCA share is 52%.

In the manuscript (Fig. 2A)

  • PVCA in R (the pvca package), with age binned in decades (0–9, 10–19, ..., 70+)
  • The study source (batch) was the largest contributor among all available metadata: 14% of the variance in BCR V-gene usage, 53% in TCR V-gene usage
  • This despite every sample being PBMC and processed with the same library preparation protocol

Differences from the manuscript

  • vAIRRify's PVCA is a Python port of pvca::pvcaBatchAssess, without its vsn2 normalisation
  • PERMANOVA isn't in the manuscript
  • TODO: check which variables Fig. 2A included (with depth, zaslavsky's batch share is 52%, close to the manuscript's 53%)

Caveats

  • PVCA needs at least two features
  • PERMANOVA's permutations treat a patient's repertoires as independent
  • With depth features, log10_clonotypes as a variable is circular
