Within-batch evaluation and SHAP (Fig. 3B)

within = design.within_batch(base, min_group_class_size=5) · vf.plots.within_batch(within) shap = design.shap(base, target=None, top=15) · vf.plots.shap(shap) · vf.plots.shap_beeswarm(shap, by="value") · vf.plots.shap_beeswarm(shap, by="batch")

What it answers

  • Within batch: does the signal hold once the batch is fixed?
  • SHAP: which features carry it?

How it works

  • All batches: the out-of-fold AUC from the baseline
  • Within batch: the same scores, judged inside mixed-label batches that hold at least min_group_class_size of both the class and the rest
  • SHAP: mean |SHAP| over out-of-fold repertoires, for one label class (by default the most recoverable one)
  • Bar colour: positively associated with the class (pink) or negatively (blue), from the sign of the correlation between a feature's value and its SHAP value
  • Beeswarms: the repertoires behind the bars, for the same top features. One dot per out-of-fold repertoire and feature, at its SHAP value: how much that feature changed the repertoire's predicted probability of the class. Dots pile up where values are similar; their height within a row means nothing else
    • by="value": coloured by the feature's value in that repertoire (pink high, blue low)
    • by="batch": coloured by the repertoire's batch, triangles for repertoires of the class. With more batches than colours, the batches whose mean prediction is furthest from the average keep a colour
    • Last row: per repertoire, the summed SHAP values of all the features not shown (others=False hides it)

Reading the result

  • A high AUC across batches that drops within batches points to a batch shortcut
  • Blank cells: no batch holds both the class and the rest
  • Zaslavsky (TRB, V genes): HIV reaches AUC 0.96 across batches but 0.65 within the HIV study
  • Batch beeswarm: one colour on the positive side of most rows points to a batch that carries the signal. Zaslavsky (TRB, V genes): the repertoires pushed towards influenza are the Flu vaccine UPenn 2021 batch. Whether that is influenza or the batch, this plot can't tell; the within-batch AUC can
  • With many features (e.g. VJ bias), the summed row can outweigh the features shown: the model then relies on many small differences rather than a few features

In the manuscript (Fig. 4)

  • The idea: a classifier that uses batch-associated signal as a shortcut looks better across batches than it is; a within-batch evaluation is invariant to such shortcuts, and closer to a clinical setting where disease and batch aren't aligned
  • The manuscript applied it to the published Mal-ID models; vAIRRify applies it to the baseline classifier
  • For HIV, T1D and lupus, and for BCR and TCR alike, within-batch AUCs were lower than across-batch ones:
    • lupus (BCR + TCR metamodel): 0.75 and 0.59 within the two mixed-label batches, against 0.98 across batches
    • HIV (TCR metamodel): 0.90 across batches, 0.61 within the mixed-label HIV study
  • Healthy repertoires from mixed-label batches scored higher for the disease than healthy repertoires from healthy-only batches

SHAP in the manuscript (Fig. 3B, S2, S3)

  • TreeExplainer (shap 0.48.0) on out-of-fold predictions
  • Influenza: repertoire size first; TRBV18 enriched and TRBV3-1 depleted relative to other datasets, "likely due to differences in sequencing library preparation"
  • In vAIRRify's zaslavsky run, TRBV3-1 comes out positively associated with influenza: after tcrio ingestion it's present in all 37 influenza repertoires but in only 223 of 542 overall (e.g. 11 of 95 HIV, 13 of 58 COVID-19). TODO: check the ingestion.

Caveats

  • Few batches are mixed-label in many merged datasets; with small classes, lower min_group_class_size
