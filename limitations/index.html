Limitations

  • Repeated measures: a patient's repertoires all stay. The baseline's random folds keep them together, but PERMANOVA's permutations treat them as independent. TODO: restricted permutations.
  • Labels: one categorical label per design. Multi-class labels are predicted one class against the rest; continuous labels aren't supported (bin them with with_metadata).
  • Mixed-label batches: the within-batch evaluation needs batches holding both a class and the rest. Merged datasets often have few.
  • Extrapolated depth: clonotypes at depths beyond a repertoire's own reads come from tcrio's extrapolation, not from measured data
  • Loci: V/J gene usage and HLA inference are TRB-only in tcrio for now
  • HLA: inferred from the repertoire, so the scores may carry dataset biases themselves
  • Technical vs biological: some V genes vary for germline reasons (see Germline variation); a predictive feature isn't technical by definition

Limitations the manuscript discusses

  • Treating broad repertoire differences between diseases (e.g. V-gene usage) as technical is an assumption: they can carry disease signal too, e.g. TRBV9+ T cells likely underlie ankylosing spondylitis
  • Some features labelled technical may be biological bystanders, such as HLA associations of a disease; such features should still predict within batches
  • Inferred HLA scores come from a model trained on another dataset; they may reflect dataset biases, and repertoire properties besides HLA
  • DNA- and RNA-based protocols sample different molecules, so a failure to transfer between them can be biological as well as technical
  • Independent cohorts differ in depth and patient characteristics (e.g. mild vs hospitalised COVID-19), which complicates any transfer
